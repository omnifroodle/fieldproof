Beat 4 · evidence you can trust

HASHED ON THE PHONE, AT CAPTURE

The photograph is resized and encoded first, then SHA-256 runs over exactly the bytes that will be stored. The hash rides on the report; the pixels ride on a separate document.

Verify is a re-computation, not a lookup

The dashboard fetches the photo bytes from App Services, hashes what came back, and compares it with what the phone recorded.

The same check runs on the phone.

Changing the picture without changing the recorded hash is the natural shape of the attack — which is exactly what this catches.

Say the limits out loud to a security-minded audience: this proves the stored photo is byte-identical to what the device hashed. It does not prove the camera saw what the metadata claims — for that you sign the hash in the Secure Enclave, which is a small addition.
